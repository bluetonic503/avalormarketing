AI-era SEO: the signals
that still matter in 2026

As AI overviews reshape how people search, the old rules don't all apply — but some fundamentals have only gotten stronger. Here's what to focus on right now.

Google's AI Overviews have changed what rank 1 means. For a growing number of queries, the answer appears before any blue links — synthesised, confident, and fully self-contained. Clicks to the first organic result are down 35% year-on-year for informational queries.

Does that mean SEO is dead? Absolutely not. It means the rules have shifted — and the brands paying attention are pulling further ahead of those still playing the 2022 game.

What's changed

AI Overviews eat informational clicks

Queries like "what is email marketing" or "how does Google Ads work" now resolve in the AI Overview box. If your content strategy is built on informational head terms, you're writing for an audience that increasingly never clicks through. This doesn't mean stop creating — it means change what you create for.

Zero-click is real — but not everywhere

Transactional queries ("SEO agency Nairobi", "email marketing tool Kenya") still drive clicks. Commercial investigation queries ("best alternatives to Klaviyo", "Avalor vs [competitor]") still drive clicks. Local queries still drive clicks. AI doesn't replace the browsing and comparison behaviour that precedes a purchase.

"The brands winning SEO in 2026 are the ones creating content that AI cites — not content that competes with it."

What still works

1. E-E-A-T is more important than ever

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. Google's AI needs sources it can trust to synthesise from. Content with real author credentials, cited data, original research, and genuine subject matter depth is being cited in AI Overviews — and that citation drives brand awareness even without a click.

2. Long-tail transactional keywords

Highly specific queries with commercial intent still resolve to organic results. "Email marketing agency for Nairobi startups" is not something an AI Overview solves fully — there's evaluation and trust-building involved. Long-tail keyword clusters around your specific service and location are more valuable than ever.

3. Technical SEO is the floor

Core Web Vitals, mobile performance, crawlability, structured data, internal linking — none of this has gone away. In fact, as content quality raises the bar, a slow or poorly structured site is a faster disqualifier than it was three years ago. Run a technical audit every quarter.

4. Entity optimisation

Google thinks in entities — brands, people, places, concepts — not just keywords. Building your brand as a recognised entity (consistent NAP, author profiles, Wikipedia-adjacent mentions, structured data) makes your content more likely to be understood and surfaced correctly by AI-driven systems.

5. Conversion-focused content

Shift your content investment toward bottom-of-funnel pages: comparison pages, case studies, service pages with specific proof, pricing pages. These convert. A well-optimised "Avalor vs [competitor]" page or a "best marketing agency in Nairobi" targeting page outperforms ten generic blog posts in commercial value.

The playbook for 2026

  • Audit your traffic for AI Overview cannibalisaton — find which informational pages have lost clicks
  • Pivot those resources toward transactional and comparison content
  • Build author profiles and demonstrate real E-E-A-T on every key page
  • Invest in original data, surveys, and research that AI systems will cite
  • Strengthen technical SEO — speed, structured data, internal links
  • Claim and optimise all entity signals: Google Business Profile, social profiles, industry directories

SEO in 2026 rewards depth, specificity, and trust more than volume and velocity. If your content strategy is still about publishing 20 shallow posts a month, it's time for a rebuild. If you'd like help auditing where your SEO actually stands, get in touch with the Avalor team.
