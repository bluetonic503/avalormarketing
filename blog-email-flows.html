5 email flows every
e-commerce brand needs
in 2026

These five automated flows generate 30–40% of our clients' email revenue completely on autopilot — and most brands are missing at least three of them.

Most e-commerce brands send a newsletter. Some send a promotional blast when they have a sale. Very few have built the automation infrastructure that turns email from a channel into a revenue engine.

Across our client base, well-built email flows consistently account for 30–40% of total email revenue — without any ongoing effort after setup. Here are the five you absolutely need, what they should contain, and the benchmarks to aim for.

"Email automation isn't about sending more. It's about sending the right message to the right person at exactly the right moment."
01
Welcome Series
Avg. open rate: 58–65% · Avg. conversion: 4–6%

Your welcome flow is the highest-leverage automation you'll ever build. It fires when someone joins your list — and these subscribers are at peak attention. A 3–5 email sequence over 7 days: brand story, social proof, your best-seller, a soft offer, then a harder deadline offer. Most brands waste this moment with a single "thanks for subscribing" email.

02
Abandoned Cart Flow
Avg. recovery rate: 8–15% of abandoned carts · ROI: 40–60×

Someone added to cart and didn't buy. They're not gone — they're hesitating. A 3-email sequence: Email 1 fires 1 hour after abandonment (reminder, no discount). Email 2 fires 24 hours later (social proof, answer objections). Email 3 fires 48 hours later (small urgency — "your cart expires tonight"). Do not jump straight to a discount — you're training your audience to abandon for deals.

03
Browse Abandonment Flow
Fires earlier in the funnel · Avg. open rate: 45–52%

Someone visited a product page but didn't add to cart. They're browsing, comparing, considering. A 2-email sequence — the first fires 4 hours after the visit ("still thinking about [product]?"), the second fires 48 hours later with a review or comparison angle. This flow captures intent that most brands completely ignore.

04
Post-Purchase Flow
Drives repeat purchase rate by avg. 28% · LTV multiplier

The purchase is the beginning of the relationship, not the end. A post-purchase flow has three phases: (1) Confirmation + delight — make them feel great about the decision. (2) Onboarding — help them get value from what they bought. (3) Cross-sell at day 14–21 — recommend a complementary product when the honeymoon feeling is still active. Most brands stop after the shipping confirmation.

05
Win-Back Flow
Recovers 8–12% of lapsed customers · Protects list health

A subscriber who hasn't opened or purchased in 90–180 days is a liability — they hurt your deliverability. A win-back flow: Email 1 at 90 days ("We miss you — here's what's new"). Email 2 at 105 days (your best offer ever). Email 3 at 120 days ("This is our last email to you — stay or go"). This recovers a meaningful percentage and cleanly removes those who don't engage, protecting your sender reputation for everyone else.

Before you build: the foundations

  • Use a proper ESP (Klaviyo, ActiveCampaign, or Brevo — not Mailchimp for automation-heavy setups)
  • Install server-side pixel tracking so you can fire flows based on browse and cart behaviour
  • Segment your list from day one — new vs. returning, buyers vs. non-buyers, by product category
  • Set up proper sender reputation — SPF, DKIM, DMARC — before you send at volume
  • Test every flow in staging before it goes live. A broken automation running for 3 months is a disaster.
