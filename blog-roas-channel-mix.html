The channel mix that drove
300% ROAS for our client in 2025

We break down the exact Meta, TikTok, and Google allocation that produced 3× return on ad spend for a Nairobi e-commerce brand over 6 months.

Six months ago, a Nairobi-based e-commerce brand came to us with a familiar problem: they were spending $8,000/mo on Meta ads and getting a 1.1× ROAS. Not losing money — but barely covering ad costs, with zero room to scale.

By month 6, they were at a 3.8× blended ROAS across three channels. Here's exactly what we changed, why we changed it, and what the numbers looked like at each stage.

Month 1–2: Fix the foundation

Before touching ad spend, we fixed attribution. The client's Meta Pixel was firing duplicate events. Their GA4 had no purchase tracking. Their ad account had 47 active campaigns — most of them starved of data, cannibalising each other's audiences.

We paused everything except their three best-performing ad sets (identified from raw platform data, not GA4), rebuilt the pixel with server-side events, set up GA4 with proper e-commerce tracking, and launched a clean campaign structure: one Advantage+ Shopping campaign, one retargeting campaign, and one prospecting campaign by audience temperature.

By end of month 2, Meta alone was at 1.9× ROAS — on less spend than before.

Month 3: Enter TikTok

The brand's best-selling products were visually satisfying — unboxing and "in use" content would work well. We launched TikTok with a $1,500/mo test budget, using three creative concepts: a problem/solution hook, a UGC-style review, and a lifestyle fit.

The UGC-style review outperformed everything — 3.2× ROAS in week 3. We scaled it to $3,000/mo. Cost per acquisition on TikTok was 44% lower than Meta for the same products.

"TikTok's algorithm rewards creative quality over audience precision. One winning creative can carry an entire account for weeks."

Month 4–5: Google captures intent

Meta and TikTok generate demand. Google captures it. We added a $2,000/mo Google Shopping + Search campaign targeting branded and category keywords. Conversion rate on Google was 4.1% versus 1.8% on Meta — because these were customers who were already looking.

The real unlock was the full-funnel effect: TikTok created awareness, Meta nurtured consideration, Google closed purchase intent. Each channel made the others more efficient.

Month 6: The blended result

  • Meta Ads: $4,500/mo spend → 3.1× ROAS
  • TikTok Ads: $3,000/mo spend → 3.6× ROAS
  • Google Ads: $2,000/mo spend → 5.2× ROAS
  • Total blended: $9,500/mo → 3.8× ROAS

Total monthly revenue from paid: $36,100. Up from $8,800 at month 1 on a similar budget.

The three lessons

1. Attribution first, always

You cannot optimise what you cannot measure. Fixing attribution before scaling budget is non-negotiable. Most brands are making decisions on broken data.

2. Channel sequencing matters

Don't add Google before Meta is profitable. Don't add TikTok before you have winning creative. Each channel should earn its budget before the next one opens.

3. Creative is the variable

The single biggest ROAS improvement came from better creative — not better targeting, not bigger budget. The winning TikTok creative took 3 hours to film and cost $0. It outperformed $5,000 of studio-produced content.
