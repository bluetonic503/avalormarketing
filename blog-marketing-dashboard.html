How to build a marketing
dashboard that actually
drives decisions

Most marketing dashboards are graveyards for vanity metrics. Here's the framework we use to build dashboards that lead to better weekly decisions.

The average marketing dashboard has 47 metrics and drives exactly zero decisions. It's a data graveyard — sessions, impressions, followers, engagement rate — all updating in real time, all completely useless for knowing what to do next Monday.

A good marketing dashboard has one job: surface the information you need to make the three or four decisions that matter this week. Here's how we build them for every Avalor client.

Step 1: Start with the decision, not the data

Before you open Looker Studio or any other tool, write down the three decisions you make every week as a marketing team. For most brands they look like: "Should we increase or decrease this channel's budget?" "Which creative is outperforming?" "Is our CPA trending in the right direction?"

Every metric on your dashboard should answer one of these questions. If a metric doesn't connect to a decision, it doesn't belong.

"A dashboard that doesn't change your behaviour is just a report. Build dashboards that change what you do on Monday morning."

The three-tier dashboard structure

Tier 1 — Business outcomes (weekly view)

Revenue, total orders, average order value, customer acquisition cost, and ROAS by channel. These are the numbers your CEO and CFO care about. They tell you if the whole machine is working.

Tier 2 — Channel performance (weekly view)

One row per channel: Meta, TikTok, Google, Email, Organic. For each: spend, revenue attributed, ROAS, CPA, and week-on-week trend. This tells you where to put more money and where to pull back.

Tier 3 — Creative & content (weekly view)

Top 5 ads by ROAS this week. Bottom 5 ads by CPA. Top organic pages by sessions and conversion rate. This tells you what's resonating and what to kill or iterate.

The metrics that actually matter

  • Blended ROAS — total revenue divided by total ad spend across all channels. The single most important number.
  • CPA by channel — cost per acquisition broken down so you know which channels are efficient
  • New vs. returning customer ratio — are you acquiring or retaining? Both matter differently
  • Email revenue % — what share of total revenue comes from email? Should be 25–40% for healthy e-commerce
  • 7-day conversion rate trend — is your site getting better or worse at converting the traffic it receives?
  • Creative fatigue score — frequency vs. CTR. When frequency goes up and CTR goes down, your creative is tired

The metrics to remove immediately

  • Impressions (vanity — only matters in context of CTR and cost)
  • Follower count (a lagging indicator of nothing actionable)
  • Reach (same problem as impressions)
  • Page views without conversion context
  • Platform-reported ROAS without cross-channel reconciliation (Meta lies. Google lies. Reconcile.)

Tools we recommend

Looker Studio (free)

Connects to GA4, Google Ads, Search Console, and most major platforms via connectors. Best for brands on a budget who want a clean, shareable dashboard without paying for software.

Triple Whale or Northbeam (paid)

Built for e-commerce, these tools solve the cross-channel attribution problem natively. If you're spending $10k+/mo across channels, the attribution clarity pays for itself many times over.

Klaviyo Analytics

For email, Klaviyo's built-in analytics are best-in-class. Don't try to replicate email data in another tool — use Klaviyo's own dashboard for email-specific decisions and feed only revenue totals into your master dashboard.
